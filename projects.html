Academic project

The Competency Multiverse — Assessment Centre Simulation

Designed a classroom-based assessment centre with 5 workplace role-play scenarios and a common 25-mark rubric, to observe and evaluate individual competencies independent of group performance.

Teams of 5 drew one of five scenarios ("universes") — a workplace conflict, a business turnaround problem, competing urgent priorities, a repeated communication failure, and a resource-constrained ethical dilemma — then had 3 minutes to prepare and 5 minutes to role-play it live.

Every participant was scored individually against five competencies (Communication, Decision-making, Problem-solving, Leadership & Teamwork, Adaptability under pressure), 5 marks each, using a shared behaviour-based rubric rather than subjective impressions.

The rubric was deliberately designed to counter common assessment biases — the loudest person in the room isn't automatically the best performer, and every score had to be backed by observed behaviour, not confidence or fluency.

Research

Beyond the Green Label: GHRM & Corporate Greenwashing

Synthesised 50 academic journals via thematic analysis to examine whether Green Human Resource Management practices drive authentic sustainability outcomes — or become a mechanism for symbolic greenwashing.

Most GHRM literature assumes that green HR practices — recruitment, training, performance management, and rewards aligned with ecological goals — automatically produce genuine environmental outcomes. This research questioned that assumption.

By synthesising existing studies, the paper examines the conditions that separate authentic sustainability from surface-level "greenwashing" — namely leadership authenticity, how deeply green practices are integrated into HR systems (versus copied from external programmes), and genuine employee empowerment rather than image-driven policy.

The conclusion: HR isn't a neutral implementer of sustainability policy — it's a decisive actor whose depth of involvement determines whether a company's environmental narrative reflects real change or a well-branded facade.
